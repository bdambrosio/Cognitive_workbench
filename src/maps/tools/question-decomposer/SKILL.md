# Question Decomposer

Transform complex, multi-faceted questions into structured sets of simpler questions that can be answered systematically.

## When to Use This Skill

- When facing broad or ambiguous questions
- When planning research or investigation
- When the question has multiple implicit parts
- When clarifying requirements or objectives

## Decomposition Process

### 1. Identify Question Type
- Factual: "What is X?"
- Causal: "Why does X happen?"
- Procedural: "How do I do X?"
- Evaluative: "Should we do X?"
- Comparative: "Which is better, X or Y?"

### 2. Find Hidden Assumptions
- What does the question assume?
- What terms need definition?
- What context is implied?

### 3. Break Into Sub-Questions
- What must be answered first?
- What are the component questions?
- What are the dependencies?

### 4. Identify Information Needs
- What facts are needed?
- What data would help?
- Who might know the answer?

### 5. Sequence Questions
- What's the logical order?
- What enables what?
- What can be answered in parallel?

## Example Decomposition

**Original Question**: "Should we adopt technology X?"

**Decomposed**:
1. What problem are we trying to solve?
2. What is technology X and how does it work?
3. What are our current capabilities?
4. What would technology X enable us to do?
5. What are the costs (time, money, complexity)?
6. What are the alternatives?
7. What are the risks?
8. How do others use technology X?
9. What's the decision criteria?
10. Given answers to 1-9, what's the recommendation?

## Output Format

- **Original Question**: [Restate clearly]
- **Key Terms**: [Definitions needed]
- **Assumptions**: [What's implicit]
- **Sub-Questions**: [Numbered list, ordered by dependency]
- **Information Needed**: [What to research]
- **Answering Strategy**: [Recommended approach]

## Audit sequence (METHOD.md §12)

1. **Receive materials** (data room, repo access, docs) and **confirm scope in writing**.
2. **Enumerate claims, then close the surface.** Identify the claim surface (README, specs, marketing, internal docs), then end enumeration with exactly:
   
   === CLAIM SURFACE ===
   <N> claims
   
   The line immediately after the marker must contain only a number followed by `claims`. The surface is then frozen; anything discovered later goes in an addendum with its own count, never into the original population.
3. **Prioritise** according to §4: safety/business-ending claims → architecture → operations → micro-claims. Verify the top N.
4. **Work the priority order straight through.** Do not pause for client confirmation; report findings in the deliverable.
5. **Continue to the defensible coverage threshold**, stopping only when the consistency rate and severity distribution of what remains make the rest low-risk.
6. **Write the report**, applying §7 to revised findings.
6b. **Verify the report against its own citations.** Every finding must cite both the document making the claim and the file/line range settling it; every reference must resolve to the actual materials. Unresolved citations do not ship.
7. **Deliver. Propose method-file edits** limited to technique, never target-specific facts; remove proper nouns from proposed method lessons. (`METHOD.md:466-537`)

## Claim-count rule

One claim is **one assertion that can take exactly one §6 verdict**. Statements that would always receive the same verdict on the same evidence count as one claim. If a statement could hold in one part and fail in another, it counts as more than one. **Every claim on the surface is counted regardless of priority**; priority controls verification order, not the denominator. Claims checked only in aggregate remain in the count and must be reported as aggregate verification. (`METHOD.md:482-489`)

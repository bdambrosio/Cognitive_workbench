22|

24|

27|verification** step — *is what they told you true?* — and that is what makes
28|it a distinct product rather than a re-skin of a code review.

30|## 1a. Level of assurance
31|
32|**This is limited assurance over a disclosed subset of the claim surface, and
33|the conclusion is expressed positively over that subset only.**
34|
35|The distinction matters because it fixes what the report means. An engagement
36|offering *reasonable* assurance examines enough to reduce risk to acceptably
37|low and concludes positively over the whole subject matter. This engagement
38|does not: §4 prioritises, §12 stops at a coverage threshold, and §11 lists
39|what is out of scope. So the conclusion in §9 says something narrower than it
40|appears to — *of the claims examined, here is how they stood up* — and the
41|coverage statement is not a footnote to it but part of it.
42|
43|**Two consequences that bind.**
44|

46|   The recommendation and the coverage line travel together, in the report and
47|   in the Gap Map. A verdict without a denominator is not a conclusion.
48|2. **Never write a sentence that implies the unexamined was examined.** "The
49|   system does what it says" claims the whole surface. "Of the 43 claims
50|   verified, 39 hold" claims what was done. §4's coverage honesty is this rule

90|
91|1. **Enumerate the claims first.** Body's README, project spec and IMU docs
92|   yielded roughly 68 micro-claims.
93|2. **Prioritise** (order below).

96|
97|**Coverage honesty.** Jill declined to write a final report at five findings
98|rather than
99|
100|> "hand you a 5-finding document that says 'everything checks out' based on
101|> 7% coverage of the claim surface."
102|
103|A report must be able to say *here is what holds, here is what does not, and
104|here is what I did not check and why it matters*. Silence about coverage
105|reads as completeness, and completeness is the one thing a due-diligence
106|report must never imply without having earned it.
107|
108|**Materiality — what makes a gap worth reporting at all.**
109|
110|A gap is material if a reasonable buyer, knowing it, **would change the price,
111|the structure of the deal, or the decision to close.** That is the threshold,
112|and it is transaction-relative rather than absolute: the same defect is
113|material in a $400k acquisition and noise in a $40m one.
114|
115|Three things follow. **Report every material gap regardless of which way it
116|pushes the price** — §10's independence rule. **A gap below the threshold is
117|not a finding**; it belongs in the consistency rate, not the findings table.
118|And **materiality is judged against the buyer's decision, not the auditor's
119|taste** — "this code is poorly organised" is not material unless it bears on
120|what was claimed.
121|
122|Without this, "load-bearing" is whatever the reporter thinks it is. Measured
123|2026-08-24: three arms on one fixture reported 6, 3 and 2 unplanted findings.
124|Part of that spread is three reporters placing an undefined threshold in three
125|different places.

128|

130|   money that cannot be collected, a dependency that can withdraw. In a
131|   physical product this tier is safety-critical mechanism; in a SaaS target
132|   it is recoverability, payment integrity and single points of control.
133|2. **Architectural invariants** — do the stated boundaries hold? Isolation,
134|   redundancy, failover, tier separation, shared state.
135|3. **Operational parameters** — retry intervals, timeouts, monitoring,
136|   retention windows, thresholds affecting day-to-day operation.
137|4. **Micro-claims** — individually low-impact; collectively they establish the
138|   consistency rate, which is the strongest available signal about the claims

275|## 6. Verdict vocabulary
276|
277|| Verdict | Meaning | Buyer impact |
278||---|---|---|
279|| `[real]` | Claim holds; implementation matches spec | None |
280|| `[real, minor caveat]` | Holds; note does not change the decision | Awareness |
281|| `[real, operational caveat]` | Holds today; operational context qualifies it | Awareness + operational planning |
282|| `[partial]` | Mostly true, with a specific citable gap | **Material** — buyer should price the gap |
283|| `[delta]` | Claim is false; the code does not do what the docs say | **Material** — buyer should revalue or walk |
284|| `[unverifiable]` | Could not be verified from available materials | Not a finding — goes in Remaining Claims with the reason |
285|| `[non-delta]` | No claim to verify (code exists, docs make no statement) | Not a finding — noted for completeness |
286|| `[derived]` | Not a claim test — a consequence computed from two or more stated figures (§5's second shape) | **As severe as the consequence.** Frequently material |
287|
288|**`[partial]` vs `[delta]` is a real distinction.** `[partial]` is "the claim
289|is 80% true and here is the 20% that is not, with a citation to the corner
290|case." `[delta]` is "the claim is false; the code does X, the docs say Y,
291|here are both citations." A buyer treats them differently: `[partial]` is a
292|known limitation to price in; `[delta]` is a broken promise that may indicate
293|a pattern.
294|
295|**`[derived]` is not a verdict on a claim, and that is the point.** The other
296|eight say how a stated claim stood up. `[derived]` says the seller's own
297|figures, taken together, entail something neither document states. It carries
298|no claim source because there is no claim — which is why it needs §5's second
299|shape rather than a row in the format above.
300|
301|**`[unverifiable]` must never be reported as `[delta]`.** *"I couldn't find
302|the code"* is not the same as *"the code isn't there."* The former belongs in
303|Remaining Claims; only the latter is a finding.
304|

306|`[delta]` 35, `[real]` 29, `[partial]` 20, `[derived]` 11, `[real, minor
307|caveat]` 11, `[unverifiable]` 8, `[non-delta]` 5, `[real, operational
308|caveat]` 4. Use the narrowest one that fits.

403|

455|## 12. Running an audit: sequence
456|
457|Launched with `scenarios/audit.yaml` — one scenario for every engagement,
458|with two per-target lines (`world_name`, `external_repo`). Its header carries
459|the operational detail; this section is the method.
460|
461|1. **Receive materials** (data room, repo access, docs). **Confirm scope in
462|   writing.**
463|2. **Enumerate claims, then CLOSE the surface.** Identify the claim surface —
464|   README, specs, marketing, internal docs — and end enumeration by stating
465|   the count after a line reading exactly:
466|
467|   ```
468|   === CLAIM SURFACE ===
469|   <N> claims
470|   ```
471|
472|   The line immediately after the marker carries the count and nothing else:
473|   a number, then the word `claims`. List the surface afterwards if it helps
474|   the client; the count comes first, on its own line, because it is the
475|   number every coverage figure in the report divides by.
476|
477|   Measured 2026-08-24, first exposure, three arms, three readings of a
478|   looser wording: one stated the count, one enumerated the surface and never
479|   counted it, and one quoted the instruction back. All three were defensible
480|   readings of "state the count after the marker", which is why the shape is
481|   now fixed rather than described.
482|
483|   From that point the surface is **frozen**. It is the denominator every
484|   coverage figure in the report is measured against, and a denominator that
485|   moves makes every percentage stated before it mean something else. A claim
486|   met later that was missed here goes in an **addendum** with its own count,
487|   never into the original population.
488|
489|   The marker does two things nothing else can. It fixes **when** enumeration
490|   ended, so the record shows verification followed it rather than running
491|   alongside it. And it fixes **what** the denominator was, so the coverage
492|   statement in the report can be checked against the number the audit
493|   actually committed to.
494|3. **Prioritise** per §4's safety → architecture → operations → micro order.
495|   Verify top N.
496|4. **Work the priority order straight through.** Do not pause for
497|   confirmation; there is no channel to the client mid-engagement. Deltas are
498|   reported in the deliverable, where the client can act on them.
499|5. **Continue to the coverage threshold where the report is defensible** —
500|   §4's "7% is not enough" rule applied in reverse: stop when the consistency
501|   rate and the severity distribution of what remains make the rest low-risk.
502|6. **Write the report.** Apply §7 to any findings that were revised.
503|6b. **Verify the report against its own citations.** Every claim resolves to
504|   a line in the materials, or it does not ship — §5 applied to the finished
505|   deliverable rather than trusted to the author. It catches a claim citing
506|   nothing, not one citing the wrong line convincingly: a floor, not a
507|   guarantee.
508|7. **Deliver. Propose method-file edits (technique only).** The working record
509|   is then copied out and the world destroyed (§14) — both by the practice,
510|   not by you.
511|
512|**There is no independent review, and that is a decision rather than an
513|oversight.** Step 6b is self-review — the author checking its own citations —
514|and the method says plainly it is a floor, not a guarantee. A second reviewer
515|who took no part in the work is what the assurance profession requires for
516|engagements of consequence, and it is not proportionate at this price point.
517|Revisit it before the first engagement where a finding moves real money; a
518|self-reviewed report is the weakest link in §10's defence.

<N> claims


The count line must contain only a number followed by `claims`; any subsequent surface list comes afterward (`METHOD.md:461-475`). From that point the surface is **frozen**. It is the denominator for every coverage figure. A claim discovered later that was missed at closure goes in an addendum with its own count, never into the original population (`METHOD.md:483-487`). The marker records both when enumeration ended and what denominator verification committed to (`METHOD.md:489-493`).

## Priority order

Within material claims, verify in this order (`METHOD.md:127-139`):

1. **Claims whose failure ends the business:** unrecoverable data, uncollectable money, or a dependency that can withdraw; for physical products, safety-critical mechanisms; for SaaS, recoverability, payment integrity, and single points of control.
2. **Architectural invariants:** whether stated boundaries hold— isolation, redundancy, failover, tier separation, and shared state.
3. **Operational parameters:** retry intervals, timeouts, monitoring, retention windows, and thresholds affecting daily operation.
4. **Micro-claims:** individually low-impact claims that collectively establish the consistency rate and provide the strongest signal about unchecked claims.

The tiers are consequence-based, not subsystem-based. If a tier is empty for a target, say so in the coverage statement rather than silently using a reduced order (`METHOD.md:141-147`). The reason for this order is that if the audit is cut short, it resolves the buyer’s most expensive unknowns first (`METHOD.md:149-152`).

## Finding format

For an ordinary claim test:

text
**Finding N: <short title> — [verdict]**

Claim (<source>, e.g. README line 201-202): <the stated claim>

Evidence: <file:lines> — <what the code actually does>

Delta: <None, or the specific gap>


Every finding must cite both halves: the document making the claim and the file/line range showing implementation. Citations are the professional standard of care and defamation defence, not decoration (`METHOD.md:154-169`). Figures must be quoted verbatim from source, never taken from summaries; numbers, dates, and thresholds require the source line and verbatim quotation (`METHOD.md:171-177`, `METHOD.md:203-206`).

For an absence, show both searches: lexical search using the claim’s own words and structural inspection of the directory where the feature would live. A search proves diligence, not by itself absence; the search terms must fit the claim (`METHOD.md:179-201`).

### Derived finding format

Some material consequences follow from multiple figures stated separately by the seller and have no single claim source:

text
**Finding N: <short title> — [derived]**

Basis: <file:lines> — <the first stated figure, verbatim>
       <file:lines> — <the second, verbatim>

Derivation: <the arithmetic, written out so a reader can check it>

Consequence: <what follows, and why a buyer cares>

Escalates: <Finding N, or None>


Derived findings may use only the target’s own verbatim numbers, dates, or thresholds; the arithmetic must be written out; no recommendation belongs inside the finding; and the finding must identify the ordinary finding it escalates, or say `None` (`METHOD.md:208-253`). If the result is date-based, state the expiry date and what happens then, and include an as-of date for the materials (`METHOD.md:255-261`). Its priority inherits the underlying facts and rises when the combined consequence is more severe (`METHOD.md:263-266`).

## Verdict vocabulary

- `[real]`: claim holds; implementation matches the specification.
- `[real, minor caveat]`: holds; caveat does not change the decision.
- `[real, operational caveat]`: holds today, with operational context requiring planning.
- `[partial]`: mostly true, with a specific citable material gap.
- `[delta]`: claim is false; code does not do what documentation says.
- `[unverifiable]`: could not verify from available material; not a finding, goes in Remaining Claims with the reason.
- `[non-delta]`: code exists but documentation makes no claim; not a finding, noted for completeness.
- `[derived]`: consequence computed from two or more stated figures, as defined above (`METHOD.md:275-286`).

`[partial]` means a substantially true claim with a known material corner-case; `[delta]` means the claim itself is false. `[unverifiable]` must never be converted into `[delta]` merely because code was not found (`METHOD.md:288-303`). Use the narrowest verdict that fits (`METHOD.md:305-308`).

## Corrections and recaps

If later evidence contradicts a finding, the auditor corrects it in the report with a one-line explanation of what changed and why (`METHOD.md:310-318`). There is no working/mid-engagement recap because the engagement has no client channel; the deliverable recap is the executive synthesis in the finished report (`METHOD.md:324-345`).

## Recommendation

Use only this taxonomy (`METHOD.md:348-356`):

- **Clear:** no deltas or notable caveats; system does what it says.
- **Clear with caveats:** no functional deltas, but documentation drift, maintenance debt, or operational notes that do not change fundamental valuation.
- **Conditional:** deltas are addressable in a sprint or two or concern a non-critical feature; proceed if seller fixes them or buyer accepts them.
- **Material:** deltas significantly change valuation or risk profile; buyer must explicitly price them in or walk.
- **Walk:** claims are systematically false or the codebase is not what documentation describes.

The audit recommends; the buyer decides. Keep recommendation vocabulary separate from buyer action vocabulary such as proceed, negotiate, or walk (`METHOD.md:358-370`).

## Liability posture and exclusions

This is expert due diligence, not legal advice: report observations, claim/implementation deltas, and professional assessment; legal and business judgement remain with the client (`METHOD.md:372-385`). Independence requires reporting material gaps regardless of which way they move price, while relying on citations, the prohibition on `[unverifiable]` as `[delta]`, the materiality threshold, and final citation verification (`METHOD.md:387-399`).

It is not a pen-test, code-quality review, design-opinion exercise, legal advice, or acceptance testing of contracted work (`METHOD.md:436-453`).

## Audit run sequence

1. Receive materials and confirm scope in writing.
2. Enumerate claims and close/freeze the surface with the exact `=== CLAIM SURFACE ===` marker and count.
3. Prioritise by safety/business-ending → architecture → operations → micro-claims.
4. Work straight through without pausing for client confirmation.
5. Continue to the defensible coverage threshold: apply the “7% is not enough” rule in reverse and stop only when the consistency rate and severity distribution of remaining claims make the remainder low-risk (`METHOD.md:455-501`). The method does **not** specify a fixed percentage threshold; defensibility depends on coverage, consistency, and remaining-severity distribution.
6. Write the report and apply corrections.
7. Verify every report claim against its own citations; anything that does not resolve to a materials line does not ship. This is a floor, not a guarantee, and catches missing citations rather than necessarily incorrect-but-plausible line references (`METHOD.md:502-507`).
8. Deliver and propose method-file edits limited to technique. The working record is copied out and the audit world destroyed by the practice/harness, not by the auditor (`METHOD.md:508-518`).

There is no independent review; step 6b is self-review, and the method acknowledges this as the weakest assurance link to revisit before an engagement where a finding moves real money (`METHOD.md:512-518`).

## Gap Map

The Gap Map is designed as a one-card, approximately 680px-wide executive artifact. Its scan order is target identity, verdict band, key items, coverage, and full-report link (`METHOD.md:620-628`). The verdict band contains the §9 recommendation, colour-coded, and is the only place the recommendation appears (`METHOD.md:630-633`).

It contains three to five mixed key items. Items have an icon, one-line claim, and two-line note; citations remain in the report (`METHOD.md:635-637`). For Clear/Clear with caveats, include positive confirmations as well as caveats; for Material/Walk, the balance naturally shifts toward gaps (`METHOD.md:639-645`).

The coverage line states how many claims were individually verified out of how many identified and why the remainder is or is not low-risk (`METHOD.md:647-650`). The footer includes the report link and the disclaimer “technical claims verification · not a pen-test, not legal advice”; omit logos, pricing, and “book a call” (`METHOD.md:653-661`).

## Final deliverable rules

The final turn contains **exactly two documents and nothing else**, produced together in this order (`METHOD.md:667-677`):

1. **The report**, aimed at 2,000 words or fewer. It must contain:
   - the recommendation using only §9 vocabulary;
   - findings worst first, each in the prescribed format and verdict vocabulary, with claim source and implementation source;
   - an explicit coverage statement identifying what was not checked and why it matters;
   - what the client should ask the seller before closing;
   - an inherent-limitations section after this exact marker:

     text
     === LIMITATIONS ===
     

     Immediately provide three always-present lines: materials examined and their as-of date; that the seller was **not consulted and has not confirmed the auditor’s reading of their own claims**; and the assurance level plus the coverage supporting it (`METHOD.md:678-699`).

2. **The Gap Map**, after this exact standalone marker:

   text
   === GAP MAP ===
   

   The marker separates the two documents and its absence means the engagement has not delivered (`METHOD.md:705-715`).

The report carries citations; the Gap Map does not. The Gap Map is the 30-second entry point and the report is the evidentiary proof; neither substitutes for the other (`METHOD.md:717-721`).

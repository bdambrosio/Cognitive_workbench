214|figures, taken together, entail something neither document states. It carries

216|shape rather than a row in the format above.

224|## 7. Correction protocol
225|
226|**The auditor corrects their own findings when subsequent evidence
227|contradicts them. Corrections are noted in the report with a one-line
228|explanation of what changed and why.**
229|
230|An auditor who never corrects has a citation trail that proves nothing: if no
231|evidence could change a finding, the citations were decoration.

234|
235|**Write for the person who decides.** They read the Gap Map in about thirty
236|seconds and open the report only if it earns the time. They will not read
237|forty cited findings: they read the recommendation, then drill into one or two
238|if it makes them curious.
239|

241|with a paragraph a non-specialist can read and explain back to a colleague.
242|Tier everything in between to that reader.

244|## 9. Report-level recommendation
245|
246|| Recommendation | Meaning |
247||---|---|
248|| **Clear** | No `[delta]` and no `[partial]` findings, and no caveats of note. The system does what it says. |
249|| **Clear with caveats** | No `[delta]` or `[partial]` findings. Documentation drift, maintenance debt or operational notes a buyer should know, which do not change the fundamental valuation. |
250|| **Conditional** | Material findings, but addressable — fixable in a sprint or two, or the feature is non-critical. Proceed if the seller will fix them or the buyer accepts the gap. |
251|| **Material** | Material findings that significantly change valuation or risk profile. The buyer must price this in explicitly. |
252|| **Walk** | Claims are systematically false, or the target is not what the seller described. |
253|
254|**The audit recommends; the buyer decides.** The audit says "Conditional: the
255|seller claims 30-day retention, the materials show 7." Whether 7 days is
256|acceptable is the buyer's call. The audit does not say "you should walk."
257|
258|**Keep the two vocabularies apart.** The five terms above describe the state
259|of the claims. *Proceed, negotiate, walk* is the buyer's action vocabulary,
260|and using it would put the audit in the business of advising on the deal (§2).
261|The Gap Map's closing line may offer a judgement — "the gaps are addressable
262|within the existing integration timeline" — if it is labelled as professional
263|judgement rather than written as an instruction.

265|## 10. Liability posture
266|
267|<!-- audience: practice -->
268|
269|**Category: expert due-diligence, not legal advice.** The distinction is
270|functional, not semantic. Legal advice is "the law requires X, your rights
271|are Y, you should do Z." Expert due-diligence is "here is what I observed,
272|here is the gap between claim and implementation, here is my professional
273|assessment of what that gap means for the value of the asset." The legal and
274|business judgement stays with the client.
275|
276|Same category as a financial auditor reporting material misstatements, a
277|structural engineer certifying an undersized wall, or a pen-test firm
278|reporting an exploitable endpoint.
279|
280|**Independence, and which way the incentive runs.** The buyer engages the
281|auditor, and the buyer benefits from findings: every delta is leverage on
282|price. That is a self-interest threat in the direction of **over-reporting**,
283|and naming it is the first safeguard. The others are already load-bearing
284|elsewhere: §5's citation requirement, which an invented delta cannot satisfy;
285|§6's rule that `[unverifiable]` is never reported as `[delta]`; §4's
286|materiality threshold, applied **regardless of which way a gap pushes the
287|price**; and §12 step 6b, which verifies the finished report against its own
288|citations.
289|
290|A finding that survives all four is not advocacy. A finding that would not
291|have been written for a seller-side client should not be written for a
292|buyer-side one.
293|
294|**Vector 1 — client sues for sloppy work.** The real risk, and the same one
295|every audit firm carries. Managed by four things:
296|
297|1. **Scope definition in the engagement letter** — "based on the materials
298|   provided; we did not have access to production systems, live databases, or
299|   the source repository." If the defect was in production but not in the
300|   materials, it is not in scope.
301|2. **Professional standard, not perfection** — what a reasonable auditor
302|   would find from the same materials. The citation trail demonstrates the
303|   work was systematic; the correction protocol (§7) demonstrates it was
304|   honest.
305|3. **Limitation of liability clause** — caps damages, excludes
306|   consequential. This makes the risk finite rather than existential.

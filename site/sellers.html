For software companies

Your materials say the software does X. Will it hold up?

Acquirers, investors and large customers read what you say about your software, then look at the code. We check your claims against your code first, privately, so you find the gaps before they do.

Who looks closely

An acquirer

Technical due diligence reads your README, documentation and deck, and checks them against the repository. A promise the code does not keep turns into a price adjustment, a new term in the agreement, or the end of the deal.

An investor

“Our proprietary model”, “built for privacy”, “production-ready”: the statements in a deck are what an investor's technical adviser is asked to confirm.

A large customer

Procurement and security review read your website, your security page and your answers to their questionnaire, and ask for evidence behind each one.

What you receive

A report written for you to act on, with a fixed shape:

PartWhat it holds
Every claimEvery statement about the software in the documents you name, listed with where it is.
What a reader would findFor each claim a reader's decision would rest on: the claim as written, the verdict, and the file and lines of your code that settle it. You decide whether to change the words or the code.
Unsettled claimsClaims your materials cannot settle, and what a reader would have to take on trust.
Claims that holdListed with their evidence, so you know which statements you can stand behind.
Questions you will be askedWhat a careful reader would put to you about the gaps.

Each gap is rated by what it would change for the reader you name at intake: an acquirer, an investor or a customer, and what they are relying on.

Private, and for you alone

The report is yours. We send it to no one. It is a check for your own use, not an independent assessment for someone else to rely on: it carries no signature and no attestation, and a buyer who wants an independent review commissions one.

The review reads the code and does not run it. Nothing is compiled or executed.

Price, and where the work runs

A claims check is priced below a buyer's claims review. The quote follows the size of the claim set and is fixed before anything begins.

The practice is in beta. For a limited number of engagements, a claims check is free: a full engagement, same scope and same report, at no fee. Write to us to ask for one.

Your repository is examined on isolated hardware the practice controls, private or rented sole-tenant, in an environment created for the engagement. None of your code is sent to any cloud model service. The engagement's files are kept for the period in the engagement letter, so you can ask about any finding on a private page, and the practice then deletes them. A free beta check is the exception: it runs on a hosted model.

How it works

01
Intake.

Which documents carry your claims, who will read them, and what that reader will rely on.

02
Review.

The claims are listed and frozen before any evidence is read, then each claim a reader's decision would rest on is tested against your code. The others are listed as not tested, with the reason.

03
Check and rate.

A second, independent pass re-reads the evidence behind every finding, and each gap is rated against the reader you named.

04
Report and questions.

You receive the report and a private page where you can ask about any finding.

The method is the one described in How it works; only the reader changes.

We ran it on ourselves

We reviewed this site's pages against our own code. Among what the review found, two statements went further than the code did. The site said every claim is tested; since we began rating claims first, only the claims a decision rests on are. And it said a person signs each report, when nothing in the code recorded who released one. We corrected the first sentence, and built the step that records who released each report.

What it is not

Not a certification, a signed opinion or an independent report for a buyer. Not a penetration test, a run of the software, or a review of code quality or architecture. Not legal advice.

The seller says the software does X.
Does it?

An independent audit of a software business's own claims against its code, for buyers of small software companies. Every finding cites the line that settles it.

See a finished audit Talk to us

What you get

Every claim, tested

The seller's README, docs and listing are read as a list of assertions. Each one is checked against the repository and gets a verdict.

Every finding, cited

A finding names the file and lines that settle it and quotes them. You can check any of it yourself.

Rated against your thresholds

Before the audit we ask what would change your price and what would end the deal. Each gap is rated against your own words.

Reviewed, then signed

An independent pass re-reads every finding's evidence. A person confirms the report and signs it.

Who it is for

Buyers of software businesses in the $1M to $5M range, and their advisers, at the point where the seller's description of the product is the main thing you have. It is the rung below a full technical due diligence: narrower, faster, and priced so it can be done before the letter of intent rather than after.

How it works

01
Intake.

A short conversation about the deal: what you are buying, what you already know, what would move the price, what would make you walk.

02
Audit.

The claims are enumerated and frozen, then each is tested against the code and documents the seller supplied.

03
Review and rating.

A second, independent pass checks every finding's evidence. Each gap is rated against your thresholds, twice, and escalated when the readings differ.

04
Report and questions.

You get the report, and a page where you can ask about any finding, in your own time, with the record behind every answer.

Machine drafts. A person confirms and signs. The report tells you which is which on every line.

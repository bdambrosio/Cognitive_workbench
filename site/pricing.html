What a claims review includes

Intake

A conversation about what you are evaluating and, for a deal, your thresholds, held in your own time over a page that fills the engagement form as you talk.

The review

Every claim in the agreed documents, tested against the repository and the materials supplied.

Check and rating

An independent re-reading of every finding's evidence, and ratings against your thresholds, replicated and looked at again where they differ.

The report

Findings with their citations, what was and was not examined, questions to put to the seller, and a signed conclusion if you ask for one.

Questions afterwards

A private page where you can ask about any finding for the life of the decision, with the record behind each answer.

The working record

Every search and every file opened, kept, so any finding can be traced back to what was looked at.

What it does not include

Running the software, load or penetration testing, a review of code quality or architecture, and legal or financial advice. Materials the seller does not supply, whatever sits on a hosted service, in a private module, or on a third party's platform, are reported as unsettled rather than guessed at.

Timing

Intake takes about a day. A review of a small target is usually back within a few days of receiving the materials; a typical business engagement within a week, with the report the day after. Attested engagements add the time for a full reading, agreed at intake.

Where the work runs

For paid engagements the repository is examined on isolated, single-tenant hardware the practice controls, in an environment created for the engagement and deleted afterwards. None of the seller's code is sent to any cloud model service, and where the seller requires it the environment is kept out of the buyer's sight as well. Your deal terms are held in the engagement file and nowhere else. A free beta review is the exception: it runs on a hosted model.

Terms, retention and the limitation of liability are set out in the engagement letter.

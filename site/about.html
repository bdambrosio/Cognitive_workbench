Bruce D'Ambrosio

Founder of Tuuyi, a small practice doing one thing: checking what a software business says about itself against what it ships.

Bruce D'Ambrosio is Professor Emeritus of Computer Science at Oregon State University and has worked in artificial intelligence for four decades. He founded CleverSet, whose research in statistical relational learning became a personalization service used by more than seventy retailers before its acquisition by Art Technology Group in 2008, and he has done technical due diligence from both sides of that kind of transaction. Tuuyi has a record of its own: its recommender ran in production on SciCast, the IARPA-funded forecasting platform at George Mason University, through 2014 and 2015, and the project's 2015 annual report describes it.

The practice is built around one idea: that the most useful applications of frontier AI are narrow ones. Not a general assistant that answers anything loosely, but a fixed procedure designed around the models for a single job, run on infrastructure the practice controls, with a person answerable for the result. Claims review is the first such application. If you have a problem of that shape, technical claims to check, a record to reconstruct, a question a general tool answers too loosely, write and describe it. We are glad to talk it through.

Attested due diligence

Bruce has been on both sides of this work: as founder, selling CleverSet to Art Technology Group, and under contract to the buyer of a French SaaS product-recommendation service, on the technical side of the diligence.

For an attested engagement he signs this:

I have read every finding and the record behind it. Each finding cites evidence that exists as quoted and supports its verdict. Every claim in the documents named at intake received a finding, and what the materials could not settle is reported as unsettled, not as false. I do not attest to the truth of unsettled claims, to the seller's conduct, to the target's value, or to anything outside the materials supplied, and nothing here is legal advice.

He stays available to your deal team through closing, on terms set at intake.

Why it is built this way

A claim is a sentence a seller wrote. Whether it is true is a matter of what the code does, and that is a question with an answer, one file at a time. We built a fixed method for asking it the same way every time, and tooling that keeps the record of what was looked at, so the report is checkable line by line and the judgement in it is a person's.
